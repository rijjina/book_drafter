# Discussion & Implications Specialist

## Mission
Elevate raw empirical data into enduring scientific insight by contextualizing findings within broader academic discourse and addressing study limitations with intellectual honesty.

## Structure: The Inverted Funnel
Follow the roadmap outlined in `../../references/imrad-standard.md`:

### Paragraph 1: Principal Findings Synthesis
- Open with a concise, non-technical recap of the main findings in direct relation to the primary research questions.
- Do not repeat exact statistical test statistics or $p$-values already stated in Results.

### Paragraph 2–4: Contextualization with Literature
- Compare and contrast findings against existing publications:
  - **Agreement:** How do current results reinforce or validate theories proposed by [@smith2023deep]?
  - **Divergence:** Where do current findings contradict previous benchmarks (e.g., [@chen2024novel])? Provide a nuanced explanation for the difference (e.g., variation in dataset diversity, sensor calibration, regularization techniques).

### Paragraph 5: Mechanistic / Theoretical Interpretation
- Explain *why* the observed phenomena occurred.
- Propose the theoretical or mathematical mechanism responsible for the observed performance advantages.

### Paragraph 6: Practical & Translational Implications
- Articulate the real-world value of the findings:
  - What does this mean for practitioners, clinical workflows, software developers, or industrial systems?
  - How can other researchers build upon this method?

### Paragraph 7: Study Limitations (Crucial for Reviewer Buy-in)
Conform to `../../references/academic-integrity-gates.md`:
- Address at least 3 concrete limitations with candor:
  1. Sample size, demographic, or dataset distribution boundaries.
  2. Potential confounding factors or unmeasured environmental variables.
  3. Computational overhead, latency, or scalability limits.

### Paragraph 8: Future Research Trajectories
- Propose 2–3 specific, actionable next steps for future studies. Avoid vague generalities like "more research is needed."

## Style Rules
- Use appropriate scientific hedging (`../../references/academic-tone-style.md`).
- Distinguish between solid empirical facts and speculative hypotheses.

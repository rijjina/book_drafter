# Thai Academic Teaching Material

Use this skill for Thai `เอกสารประกอบการสอน` or `เอกสารคำสอน` derived from lecture
transcripts, slides, course outlines, OBE/TQF documents, and instructor evidence.

For new prose or cross-course adaptation, read
[references/generalized-teaching-writing-style.md](references/generalized-teaching-writing-style.md).
It captures reusable rhetorical traits from legacy teaching documents without retaining
their course content. Use
[assets/generic-teaching-chapter-template.md](assets/generic-teaching-chapter-template.md)
when a domain-neutral chapter scaffold is useful.

## Preserve the declared deliverable

Record exactly one document type before drafting. Do not treat
`เอกสารประกอบการสอน` and `เอกสารคำสอน` as interchangeable. Follow the current
institutional checklist when supplied; otherwise state that the structure is a working
academic default, not an official institutional ruling.

## Workflow

1. Inventory source files and identify transcript, slide, course-outline, OBE/TQF,
   assignment, and prior-format evidence.
2. If legacy documents are supplied as a style reference, extract only transferable
   traits: stance, paragraph rhythm, explanation sequence, heading logic, terminology
   handling, use of examples, tables, activities, summaries, and questions. Do not
   carry over subject claims, specialist vocabulary, examples, citations, course codes,
   people, institutions, or learning outcomes.
3. Map every planned section to source file and page/slide/marker. Mark unsupported
   required material as `MISSING DATA`; never fabricate course use, learning outcomes,
   facts, citations, or permissions.
4. Convert spoken Thai into formal written Thai while preserving technically useful
   explanations, examples, sequence, and the instructor's sound teaching voice.
5. Remove filler, repetition, classroom logistics, and live Q&A unless they clarify a
   teaching decision.
6. Organize major topics as concept, mechanism/components, worked example or learning
   activity, summary, and aligned questions/exercises where appropriate.
7. Include the required front matter, course/chapter metadata, learning objectives,
   outcome alignment, main content, examples, summary, exercises, references, and a
   source-mapping appendix when supported by the selected document type and governing
   standard.
8. Use true Word heading styles and consistent numbering even when a legacy reference
   simulated headings with bold Normal paragraphs. Preserve the writing voice, not old
   formatting defects.
9. Use a document-specific capability for DOCX production. Render pages and inspect
   Thai fonts, tables, captions, page breaks, headers/footers, numbering, and overflow.

## Content firewall for cross-course use

- Style evidence may change how prose is organized and expressed; it is never evidence
  for the new subject.
- Build definitions, examples, cases, activities, citations, and assessment questions
  from the new course's verified sources.
- Replace every legacy-specific placeholder before delivery. If current-course evidence
  is absent, keep `MISSING DATA` rather than adapting an old fact to a new discipline.
- Do not imitate accidental inconsistency. Mandatory institutional rules, factual
  accuracy, accessibility, and citation integrity override the legacy baseline.

## Default chapter components

- chapter title and course context;
- learning objectives and applicable CLO alignment;
- reading roadmap;
- logically numbered content sections;
- examples or learning activities;
- chapter summary;
- aligned exercises or review questions;
- references and source/rights notes;
- transcript/slide/source mapping appendix.

Do not add CLO/PLO/OBE/TQF material when it is irrelevant to the selected document or
unsupported by the course record.

## Output report

State the selected document type, source files used, mapping coverage, QA performed,
remaining evidence gaps, and final artifact paths. If page rendering is unavailable,
disclose that visual QA is incomplete.
